How to play

Select
Tap a unit. Double-tap to grab every unit of that kind on screen. Hold your finger still, then drag, to draw a selection box (or turn on the box button at left).
Command
With units selected, tap a tree, mine, bush, animal or fish to gather, an enemy to attack, a foundation to build, a field to farm, or open ground to move.
Camera
Drag with one finger to pan. Pinch to zoom. Tap the minimap to jump.
Building
You can build anywhere you have explored, as long as the ground is clear. Docks go on water by the shore.
Walls
Wall pieces join up into one continuous wall, with posts at corners and junctions. To add a gate to a wall you already built, pick Gate (stone) or Palisade Gate (wood) and tap or drag over the wall: the gate replaces those pieces. Stone gates have an iron portcullis that rises when your units come near and drops behind them; palisade gates have two wooden doors that swing open. Locked gates stay shut. Pick a wall or palisade, then drag along the ground from where it starts to where it ends. Lift your finger and your selected villagers build it. A short tap places a single piece; press Place to build it.
Phases
Village, Town, City and Kingdom. To advance, build enough buildings (5 for Town, 4 Town-phase ones for City, 8 for Kingdom) and research the next phase at the town center. Your buildings start as thatch and timber huts and get better with each phase: timber framing, then stone and tile, then grand royal architecture. City and Kingdom also make every building tougher, and Kingdom unlocks the best upgrades.
Citizen soldiers
Spearmen, archers, swordsmen and crossbowmen can also gather wood, stone and gold and build, a little slower than villagers. Scouts hunt fast.
Capturing
Melee soldiers attacking a building capture it instead of smashing it. Units garrisoned inside slow the capture. Rams and trebuchets destroy buildings. Walls can only be destroyed.
Fields and food
Fields never run out and hold 5 farmers. Hunt deer, sheep and boar (careful, boar fight back). Fishing boats catch fish from the dock.
Defending
Garrison archers or other soldiers on stone walls and gates (2 per piece) and they shoot from the battlements. Select a gate to lock it; a locked gate stops everyone, your own units too. Spiked Palisades (town center) hurt enemies next to your walls, and at the castle, Boiling Oil scalds enemies next to towers, castles and gates while Murder Holes add an arrow and range. Repairs cost half the building's price for the health restored. Turn on Auto-repair at the town center and nearby villagers fix damaged buildings once the fighting there stops.
Seasons and night
Each season lasts 4 minutes. Autumn fields give 10% more food; in winter fields and berries give 25% less, so stock up. Night falls every 6 minutes: your units see less, and buildings glow with torchlight. You can turn this off on the setup screen.
Building several things
Villagers already building keep a to-do list: place more buildings with the same villagers and they build each one in turn instead of dropping the first. When their list is done they help with any other unfinished building nearby, then go back to what they were doing.
Corrals
Select villagers and tap a finished corral: up to 3 herd and butcher there, bringing back food without end, like a field. Sheep can still be trained there to hunt.
Allies
Pick 1 or 2 allies on the setup screen; with allies, the rivals team up too. Allies share their sight with you, come running when you are attacked, and sometimes send you resources when you run low. Tap the 🤝 button to send them resources or ask them to attack where you are looking or to defend your town.
Navy
Docks build Fishing Boats, Transport Cogs, War Galleys, Fire Ships and Demolition Ships. To carry an army, select land units and tap your cog: they board (up to 10) and the cog comes to the nearest shore. Then select the cog and tap the far shore to land them, or press Unload. If a cog sinks, everyone aboard is lost. Fire Ships burn other ships at close range; Demolition Ships ram a ship or shore building and explode.
Hills and cliffs
Most maps have raised ground, shown lighter with a shaded edge. Units on higher ground than their target deal 25% more damage, and archers and other ranged units reach 1 tile farther; attacking uphill deals 15% less. Rocky cliffs along some hill edges block movement, so their gaps make good choke points.
Unit upgrades
At the barracks, archery range and stable you can upgrade whole unit lines: Spearman → Pikeman → Halberdier, Swordsman → Man-at-Arms → Long Swordsman, Archer → Elite Archer, Scout Rider → Light Cavalry, Knight → Cavalier → Paladin. Every unit of that type you already have is upgraded too. The castle offers Elite for your unique unit. Open Tech tree from the menu to see everything your civilization can get.
Battle tactics
Cavalry that charge in from a run deal almost double damage on the first hit. Melee units hit 20% harder against an enemy busy fighting someone else, and 25% harder against one running away. Badly hurt soldiers who are heavily outnumbered flee home (white flag); units on Hold stance never flee.
Weather
With seasons on, rain and storms roll in now and then. Rain slows units a little and weakens arrows; storms also slow ships a lot.
Roads
Stone Roads (Economy tab, 3 stone per tile) are laid by dragging, like walls. Units move 35% faster on them and choose roads when finding their way, so pave the paths between your town center, fields, mines and gates.
Farms
Fields within 3 tiles of a farmstead give 15% more food, so build farmsteads in the middle of your fields.
Victory modes
Conquest: destroy every rival. Wonder: also win by keeping a Wonder standing for 10 minutes. King of the Hill: hold the monument in the middle of the map with only your soldiers nearby for 5 minutes. Regicide: everyone starts with a King; lose him and you lose. Last Castle: everyone starts with a castle; when your last castle falls, you are out.
Relics
Golden relics lie around the map. Select a monk and tap a relic to carry it to your monastery; each relic stored there gives 30 gold a minute. If the carrier dies or the monastery falls, the relic drops.
Achievements
Earn 15 achievements across your games. See them from the title screen.
Tutorial and missions
The tutorial is optional: tap Tutorial on the title screen any time, or close it with ✕. Missions are set challenges: Hold the Castle against 15 minutes of raids, Rebuild the Village after it burned, and the Siege of the Red Canyon against a walled-in rival.
Formations
With soldiers selected, tap Form to switch between Loose, Line, Box and Wedge. In a formation, melee troops go in front, archers and monks behind, siege at the back, and the group marches at the speed of its slowest unit.
Rivals
Each computer rival has a play style: Aggressive attacks early, Builder grows a big economy, Defender fortifies with towers, Balanced does a bit of everything. Rivals raid your villagers with fast units, pull back beaten armies to regroup, and break through walls and gates with soldiers and rams. You see their styles on the end screen.
Stances
Aggressive chases enemies, Defensive stays near its post, Hold ground never moves, Passive never fights. Attack-move fights anything on the way. Patrol: tap Patrol, then a spot, and your soldiers walk back and forth between where they are and that spot, fighting any enemy they see and then going back to patrolling.
Ranks and heroes
Soldiers become Advanced and then Elite as they fight. Each civilization has a hero trained at the castle in the City phase whose aura helps nearby troops.
Trade
Build two markets far apart and send trade carts between them for gold. Markets also let you buy and sell food, wood and stone.
Win
Destroy every rival's town centers, castles and workers. With the Wonder option on, a finished wonder that stands for 10 minutes also wins.
Villages
Free villages dot the map. Keep soldiers right next to one (with no enemies there) until its bar fills and it is yours: it sends you a steady trickle of food, wood and gold. Rivals can take it the same way.
Mercenaries
Bring any unit next to a mercenary camp and tap the camp to hire foreign soldiers for gold. Each camp has 3 offers; new recruits arrive every 3 minutes.
Wolves
Wolf packs roam the wilds and attack anyone who strays close, especially villagers. Tap a wolf with soldiers selected to hunt it; nearby soldiers also step in on their own.
Fire and wells
A building below 30% health catches fire and keeps burning until it is repaired above half. A well stops fires within 7 tiles and makes nearby fields 10% faster.
Diplomacy
Rival envoys turn up now and then: a stronger rival may demand gold for peace, a weaker one may offer a truce, and one rival may ask to join you against another. Tap 🤝 to send rivals gold (it delays their attacks) or ask for a 5-minute truce.
Sea trade
Merchant ships (from the dock, Town phase) sail on their own between your dock and the farthest other dock, yours or an ally's, earning gold. Allied docks pay 40% more.
Your kingdom
On the title screen, tap Your kingdom to name your realm and design a coat of arms (shield, colors, pattern and emblem). It flies over your town centers and castles. Your career record (wins, streaks, fastest victory, favorite civilization and more) lives there too.
Campaign
The Crown of Ashford: five story chapters that unlock one after another, from a burned hall to the final battle for the crown.
Daily challenge
A new fixed setup every day (civilization, map, rivals and a twist). Win it to grow your daily streak; your fastest win of the day is kept.
Watch a battle
Spectator mode: the computer plays every kingdom on the map you picked while you watch, pan around and change the speed.
Photo mode
From the pause menu: the game freezes and the buttons hide. Frame the view, pick a filter and tap Take photo to save or share a picture stamped with your coat of arms.
Encyclopedia
Every unit, hero, building, technology and civilization with costs and stats. Open it from the title screen or the pause menu.
Voices
Units answer in their civilization's language when you select or command them, using your phone's voices. Turn them off in Settings.
Shallows and sea crossings
Boats can sail through shallows and fords on every map, and land units can still wade across them. On Four Corners there are no shallows at all, so every attack on another island goes by transport cog. The computer players ferry their armies the same way.
Living towns
Chickens peck around farms and houses, children play by the town center, shoppers browse market stalls, townsfolk stroll your roads, and windows glow at night. Battery saver turns the townsfolk off.
Sound
Waves, birds, crickets, wind, forges and market chatter come from whatever is on screen. The music builds when a battle breaks out near you, with horns and war drums, and settles when it ends.
Frozen winters
With seasons on, rivers, fords and narrow channels freeze in winter. Armies can march across the ice but ships can't sail through it. In spring the ice breaks and anyone still on it scrambles ashore. Wide seas never freeze.
Battle effects
Catapult hits and collapsing buildings shake the screen and throw debris (your phone buzzes when your own buildings are hit). Armies marching in formation carry a banner, and big ones bring a drummer.
Veterans
Soldiers earn experience by fighting. At Veteran rank they get a name, then become Elite, and finally a Champion with a gold star and an epithet ("Fergus the Bold"). Each rank adds attack, health and armor. Kills are counted on the unit panel, you are told when a named veteran falls, and your greatest champion is kept in your career record.
Sieges
Siege Towers (Siege Workshop, City phase): select one and tap an enemy wall. It rolls up and docks, and your soldiers can then walk over that stretch of wall. Siege Ladders (Barracks, City phase) let your foot soldiers climb enemy walls anywhere, slowly. Rams are pushed by a crew, damaged gates splinter, and wrecked buildings leave rubble for about 10 minutes.
Events
Every few minutes something happens: bandit raids (tap them with soldiers; each one killed near your troops pays 20 gold), plague (a Monastery softens it), harvest festivals, droughts, merchant caravans, new gold veins, and wandering heroes who offer their sword for gold.
Court and chronicle
Your ruler has a name and an age, may have an heir, and can die and be succeeded. The pause menu has 👑 Court and 📜 Chronicle, a running history of your reign (battles, phases, villages, alliances, lost and raised buildings) that you can also read from the end screen. Three advisors (Steward, Marshal, Chancellor) speak up with tips; turn them off in Settings.
Conquest
A world map of 15 lands shared with three rival kingdoms and free lands. Tap a land next to yours and attack it to play a battle on its terrain; win to take it. Every land you hold sends extra resources into your battles. Between your battles the rivals expand, fight each other, and march on your borders: defend, or the land falls. Take every rival capital to win; lose your own and the conquest ends. Progress is saved.
Pass-and-play
On the new game screen set Players on this phone to Two. Player 2 takes over the first rival kingdom (other rivals and allies stay computer-run). Each turn lasts the time you pick; when it runs out the game pauses on a cover screen so the next player can take the phone. Each player sees only their own explored map. The last human kingdom standing wins.
Home screen
The home menu fits any screen. Tap New game to pick your civilization, map and options, then Start game.
Special abilities
Selected troops show ability buttons with a cooldown: Charge (cavalry: faster, harder first hit), Shield wall (infantry: much more armor, slower), Volley (archers: arrows hit an area and harder), Healing aura (monks), Brace (mangonels and trebuchets: more range but rooted). Heroes have Inspire (nearby troops hit harder and are healed) or Battle cry (nearby enemies flee). The computer uses them too. Mixed armies now show their battle commands instead of the build menu.
Smarter rivals
Computer kingdoms now study your army and train counters (spears against cavalry, cavalry against archers), build siege towers and research ladders against walls, raise towers if you rush them early, ferry armies by ship whenever there is no land route, and use their units' special abilities. A new Brutal difficulty gives them a faster economy and bigger, earlier attacks. Rivals also taunt you, boast, and beg for mercy (turn this off with Advisors in Settings).
Iron and horses
New deposits on every map: iron ore (dark rocks with rust-red veins) and wild horse herds. Villagers gather them like stone and gold: iron goes to a storehouse or town center, horses to a farmstead or town center. Cavalry needs horses; armored soldiers, rams, trebuchets and armor upgrades need iron. Short on them? You pay extra gold instead (4 gold per iron, 5 per horse). Each kingdom has a deposit of each nearby, and more lie in the middle of the map.
Close-ups
You can now zoom in much further. Up close, units are drawn sharper, your soldiers wear your coat of arms, and named veterans show their names.
Aftermath
Fallen soldiers leave weapons, shields, helmets and dropped banners on the field for a few minutes, and crows gather after big battles.
Weather events
Storms bring lightning that can strike and set fire to buildings. Spring and autumn can bring thick fog that cuts everyone's sight. A spring storm can flood the fords and shallows so they can't be waded for a while (boats still sail them).
Building interiors
Select one of your buildings and tap Look inside to see life within: a family at the hearth, the great hall, the king on his throne, soldiers drilling, the blacksmith at his anvil, market stalls, monks at prayer, horses in the stables and more.
Spies
Train a Spy at a Market or Castle. Enemy soldiers and towers ignore spies unless they get very close. Select a spy and tap an enemy town center, market or castle to steal gold, or any other building to set it on fire. Afterwards the spy is exposed for 15 seconds and must wait a minute before striking again. Rivals send spies too.
Supply lines
Soldiers more than about 30 tiles from your town center, castle, barracks, tower, outpost, dock or a claimed village run out of supply. They show a crossed-out bread icon, hit 15% weaker, and slowly lose health down to half. A Supply Wagon (town center) keeps everyone within 12 tiles fed.
Morale
Troops that see their comrades cut down, or that are badly outnumbered and hurt, can break and run home. A hero or banner-bearer nearby keeps them steady and rallies runners. You can also select fleeing troops and tap Rally (once every 30 seconds per soldier).
Building upgrades
Select a building to upgrade it on its own: House to Manor (+5 population), Sentry to Fortified Watchtower and Stone Tower to Fortified Tower (more arrows and range), Town Center to Keep, Storehouse and Farmstead to Guild Halls (villagers nearby gather 10% faster), Barracks, Range and Stable to Drill Yards (train 25% faster), and Market to Grand Bazaar (+50 gold a minute). Upgraded buildings get a stone border and a gold pennant.
Prisoners and ransom
Enemy heroes and Champions defeated by your troops are often taken prisoner instead of killed. In the 🤝 screen you can demand ransom, or release them in exchange for a truce; rivals also send envoys offering gold. If they capture yours, pay to bring them home.
Faith and cathedrals
Relics in your monasteries, monasteries themselves and monks build ✝ faith over time. Select a Monastery to spend it on miracles: Blessing (soldiers fight 15% harder for 40 seconds), Healing Light, or Pilgrims (+120 gold). In the City Phase a Monastery can be rebuilt as a Cathedral: relics inside give double gold and faith, and it can call a Holy Day that calms all unrest and repairs your buildings.
Unrest and rebellions
Your people's mood is shown in the 🤝 screen. Hunger, plague, drought, heavy losses and buildings far from a Town Center or Castle raise unrest; monasteries, markets, festivals and blessings calm it. At 100 the peasants rise up around your most distant building. Pay the rebel leaders, or send soldiers to crush them.
Royal marriages
When you face two or more rivals, you can propose a marriage from the 🤝 screen (300 gold dowry). If they accept, they become your permanent ally. Rivals who fear your army may also offer a match themselves.
Saving
The game autosaves every 30 seconds. Open the menu and tap Save game to keep up to 3 more saves; load any of them from Load game on the title screen.
Settings
Larger buttons, a left-handed layout (minimap and buttons swap sides), graphics quality (Battery saver runs cooler and longer) and vibration.
Map editor
Paint land, water, shallows, sand, rock, forests, gold, stone, berries, fish and hills, then place 2 to 4 start spots. Save it and it shows up in the Map list; Play starts a game on it right away. Share copies a map code a friend can paste into Import.
Desktop
Left-click or drag to select, right-click to command, wheel to zoom, WASD or arrows to pan.
